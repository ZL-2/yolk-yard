# Better Together

The lobby party service uses the existing relay at `/social`. Browser-session identities authenticate reconnects with opaque tokens held in session storage; they are not registered accounts or a friends database. The service owns invitations, privacy, leader selection, readiness and matchmaking reservations. A party persists across room changes. Its membership is separate from the host-authoritative match roster and match-local team numbers.

Room discovery retains the 32-contestant Royale capacity, 16 connected human contestant limit, four late spectators and eight-player arenas. Duos adds a `teamSize` option, while `duoFill` controls a solo player's teammate independently from `fill`, which controls contestant bot fill. Public matchmaking reserves the whole group; private custom rooms can be joined by their code with the party. Admission tickets are one-use per peer and destination, expire, and are resolved by the relay before the host sees them. Clients cannot submit their own party identity through their profile. Solo selections reject a party of two. Team Scramble preserves invited pairs too.

Invitations expire after one minute. Disconnects can resume the same identity for 90 seconds; queued parties cancel if a member disconnects. Invitations, names and membership are not stored in analytics. The online list describes current sessions, not verified friends. There is no external messaging or contact upload. Server restart creates a fresh lobby session. Match host migration continues to use the existing recovery checkpoint, including teams.

The existing host-controlled 60-second Spawn Island countdown remains in use. Every configured contestant seat occupied by real humans triggers early departure. Bots never satisfy this threshold. At the cutoff, incomplete invited groups spectate together rather than being split across contestant/spectator status. The standalone offline-bot menu is removed; the explicit offline simulation setting remains for development diagnostics and its existing 10-second test flow.

Duos uses immediate elimination, with spectating restricted to a surviving teammate. There is no knock/revive mechanic. A Duo receives its placement when its final member is eliminated; both members win when their team is the last standing. Friendly damage is rejected before shields are consumed. Bots use the same team rules, per-weapon accuracy and damage as players, and share only recent observed threats.

## Combat reference and original balance

Epic's [Shooting Test 1 (February 2018)](https://www.fortnite.com/news/shooting-test-1) documents still, settled ADS first-shot accuracy, headshot critical modifiers and weapon-dependent falloff. Its listed multipliers and exceptions were specific to that test. Epic's [v11.20 update](https://www.fortnite.com/news/battle-royale-update-whats-new-in-11-20) later adjusted a shotgun headshot modifier. [Chapter 6 Season 1](https://www.fortnite.com/news/fight-as-a-ronin-in-fortnite-battle-royale-chapter-6-season-1-hunters) describes traditional weapons returning to hitscan. These are design references, not a claim of one timeless Fortnite ruleset.

Yolk Yard's fictional values are original. `src/combat.js` owns all eleven profiles, rarity scaling, critical regions, piecewise falloff, movement spread, bloom, first-shot recovery, recoil and per-pellet/build damage. Normal shell hits have no extra torso, limb or edge modifier. The upper shell above 1.32 map units on a normal 1.84-unit egg is critical; hats and cosmetics never change that region. Launchers do not receive a critical modifier. Each pellet carries only its own configured damage. Pellets use a seeded radial distribution and share one feedback group.

Most blasters use authoritative hitscan. Needle keeps a fast traveling bolt with mild game drop; Thumper and poppers retain their established projectile paths. The host creates all trajectories, applies current world cover, and validates ammo/cadence. Hitscan target history is bounded to 180 milliseconds, cannot rewind teleports, and never accepts client damage. Guests immediately predict muzzle flash, recoil and audio; only accepted host paths create tracers and hit numbers. Weapon-specific angular spread drives the reticle. Scoped Needle, Anchor and Peeper retain stable ADS while moving, with their distinct recoil and optics. Other ADS weapons retain movement and airborne penalties.

History is bounded to ten poses per contestant, omitted from recovery replication, and reacquired after host transfer. Shot time follows the actual interpolated remote pose, within the host's compensation bound. Hitscan removes the previous stream of automatic-weapon projectile entities. Existing lossless snapshot deltas and staggered bot scans remain in use. Nearby human teammates taking damage generate approximate, expiring bot callouts; they do not reveal an enemy's continuously updated position. All model artwork, eggs, lobby environment and UI are original Yolk Yard assets.

Damage feedback aggregates a shot's pellets while keeping shield and health totals separate: blue diamond numbers for shield, white health numbers, and emphasized critical marks. Only confirmed host events create these numbers. The fixed screen size remains independent of distance.

The relay uses native WebSocket ping/pong to check connected sockets during expensive scene loads. Client timeout polling recognizes a delayed local renderer and gives queued replies a chance to arrive. Relay-hosted matches transfer authority when the relay closes the host channel, not when a delayed game reply competes with a still-owned room address. Truly silent sockets still expire; disconnect and host recovery rules remain bounded. The focused browser check covers full-size desktop and phone lobby layouts, then runs two simultaneous gameplay views at 960 × 540 on the CI software renderer.
