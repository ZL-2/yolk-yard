# Operation Blackglass — coastal mission

Version 6.12.0 / Update 198. The only application entry is **Owner Controls → Campaign lab → Open Blackglass**. Launch rechecks the existing server-authenticated owner session. It has no public mode, queue, room link, reward or leaderboard entry. The JavaScript and artwork are static website assets; this is an owner-gated application feature, not encrypted content distribution.

The player infiltrates Station Seven, cuts its security feed, clears the yard, breaches operations, recovers civilian evacuation routes from the archive, disables the uplink and extracts by helicopter. Six objectives, five encounter groups, 20 original patrol operators and a finite debrief. Difficulty, volume, device radio voice, resolution and sensitivity are configurable. Subtitles remain available without speech synthesis.

WASD move, mouse look, left click fire, right click aim, Shift sprint, C/Ctrl crouch, Space jump, R reload, 1/2 swap, hold E interact, G grenade, X dressing, F flashlight, N night vision, Escape pause. Health recovers after breaking contact. Cover blocks shots, enemy vision and blast damage. Crouching reduces detection. Enemies perceive, investigate, search, coordinate, use cover and traverse a layered navigation graph.

The mission has separate original geometry, weapons, UI and sound presentation. It dynamically imports only after owner launch, pauses the existing lobby render loop, and disposes its scene, audio, listeners and GPU resources on exit. A party match launch closes it cleanly. Simulation and progress are local: relay lag does not drive this mission. Checkpoints capture objective progress and defeated patrol members; continuing reloads that checkpoint with a fresh field loadout. Storage failures retain an in-session checkpoint. A completed mission clears its checkpoint.

Performance work: static props merge by material and spatial section; repeated operators share mesh geometry; particles use a fixed pool; all agents reuse one layered navigation graph; perception and HUD update at bounded rates; desktop rendering resolution is capped. No mobile controls or multiplayer systems were added to the campaign. This is a compact proof of a campaign, not a claim of AAA cinematic or photorealistic production parity. Procedural character animation and device-generated radio speech remain visible limitations.

## Update 198 — coastal facility rebuild (6.12.0)

The eight concept sheets have been reconciled into `campaign-layout.js`: a 160 × 220 m site coordinate frame with a winding southeast approach, gate at +4 m, logistics/operations at +12 m, an actual upper command floor at +16 m, roof at +20 m, western dish terrace at +16 m, service block and eastern 48 m mast, and a northern +20 m extraction pad. The 28 m dish uses a paraboloid, radial ribs, lattice bracing, feed struts, pedestal, bearing and service platforms. Concrete joints, windows, drains, railings, wet ground, consoles, racks, generators, HVAC, cargo and a truck give each space a purpose. Some blueprint details were moved to preserve continuous travel and practical encounter lanes; these are not traced image dimensions.

The six objectives now traverse the actual facility: gate feed → logistics breach → upper command override → ground-floor archive → western dish control → northern extraction. Both internal stairs and both external service ramps are physical traversal routes. Shared surfaces and collider definitions drive rendering and a layered navigation graph; objective positions include height so a terminal cannot be used through a floor. Supplies replenish health and ammunition once per checkpoint visit. Checkpoint v2 deliberately rejects the old map's positions/objective indexes.

The 36-second introduction is rendered in the actual scene: coastal establishing move, road approach, dish orbit, operations interior and insertion handoff. It includes subtitles, device-radio narration, letterboxing, fades, skip, pause/resume and explicit mouse capture on entry. Combat and mission time remain frozen during the film. Restarting a checkpoint does not force a replay.

### Enemy behavior research and implementation

Primary material reviewed:

- [Infinity Ward, Jocelyn Chew AI developer diary](https://www.infinityward.com/news/2020-04/DevDiary_JocelynC): describes event and light reactions, hunting, disengagement and nonlinear stealth in Going Dark, and the importance of animation/design collaboration. Blackglass uses directional perception, light/crouch detection modifiers, sound events, remembered sightings, bounded searching and return to patrol.
- [Infinity Ward's Vince Zampella, developer Q&A](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/call-of-duty-qanda/1100-6026083/): describes cover, suppression and squad movement. Blackglass uses reserved cover points, crouch/peek cycles, shot suppression, distinct flanker/support roles and teammate line-of-fire checks.
- [Official Modern Warfare II announcement](https://www.callofduty.com/blog/2022/06/call-of-duty-modern-warfare-II-announcement-release-reveal): frames AI in terms of tactical awareness and cover maneuvering. The implementation scores cover relative to the last known threat and changes decisions when cover becomes exposed.
- [Activision / Michal Drobot, engine interview](https://newsroom.activisionblizzard.com/p/new-call-of-duty-tech-engine-unleashing): prioritizes visibility, latency and performance. This informed bounded AI work, local light reuse and readable interior lighting, rather than adding unbounded light or particle counts.

These are original implementations inspired by publicly described behavior, not recovered Call of Duty source code or a claim to reproduce its complete AI. Flankers react to supporting teammates and visible reload opportunities; isolated squads share only recent local sightings. Enemies cannot update a hidden player's position from the live player transform. A lost contact is searched near its remembered location before disengagement. Shots are checked against cover again at firing time. Enemies reload finite magazines, seek cover under suppression, avoid nearby grenades and may throw a telegraphed grenade at a prolonged visible position. Accuracy/reaction delay and firing slots preserve recovery opportunities. No machine learning, online service, unlimited precision or behind-wall player tracking is used.

### Multiplayer boundaries and validation

No multiplayer source, public bot, server, protocol, queue, reward or invitation logic is modified by this update. Campaign JS/CSS remain dynamically loaded through the existing authenticated owner entry. The normal client render loop is paused while the owner is in campaign; no campaign socket is created. Exit removes the campaign's listeners, animation loop, scene, audio and GPU resources; the existing party-launch handler closes it before a multiplayer launch.

Focused checks include all six routes traversed with the real collision movement, doors and objective gates, stacked-floor access, perception occlusion, memory/search expiry, suppression/reloading/grenade decisions, squad isolation, intro timing, and a dependency audit that rejects campaign imports of multiplayer simulation/network modules. Desktop acceptance runs an unrelated two-player FFA room during the campaign and checks continued snapshots, identical room/match identity and no host transfer; it also checks return to a private Box Fight. CI screenshots show actual in-engine frames, not the key art. Software-renderer timings are not evidence of a particular frame rate on a user's Mac.

Static meshes merge by material and spatial cell. The navigation graph builds on stage changes, with capped path searches and perception at ~7 Hz. Four scene lights are recycled near the player; particle pools, cached shadow maps and shared model geometry keep work bounded. This remains an original browser campaign with procedural models/animation and optional device speech, not AAA production parity.
